105: Wardley Mapping

June 16, 2026

While Disruptive Innovation tells us how attackers win and Blue Ocean Strategy tells us where to look for new value, neither framework provides a topographical map of the battlefield. This is where Wardley Mapping, pioneered by Simon Wardley, becomes a critical tool for transition management.

Strategy without a map is merely intuition. Wardley realized that business strategy was severely lacking situational awareness. In military history, generals who understand the terrain usually defeat generals who don’t. Wardley Mapping provides this visual terrain for business, allowing leaders to see how components within a value chain are evolving and where capital should be allocated to trigger a transition.

The Axes of Evolution

A Wardley Map is anchored by two key dimensions:

  1. Y-Axis (Visibility/Value Chain): How visible is the component to the end user? High visibility means the customer touches it directly. Low visibility means it’s buried deep in the technical infrastructure.
  2. X-Axis (Evolution): This is the heart of the map. Everything in a market evolves from left to right through four stages, driven by supply and demand competition.

The four evolutionary stages are:

  • Genesis: Brand new, uncertain, highly chaotic, constantly changing.
  • Custom Built: Useful, but must be built specifically for each use case.
  • Product (+ Rental): Increasingly standardized, available off-the-shelf.
  • Commodity (+ Utility): Highly standardized, ordered by volume, invisible, like electricity.

Suggested Reading:

  • Wardley Mapping: Topographical intelligence in business by Simon Wardley. A profound exploration of how to visualize business ecosystems and anticipate industrial shifts.

Transition Through Evolution

The core insight of Wardley Mapping is that as components inevitably evolve into commodities, they enable higher-order systems to be built on top of them. When computing power evolved from custom-built mainframes to a commodity utility (Cloud Computing via AWS), it dramatically reduced the cost of failure. This allowed an explosion of Genesis-level innovation (startups, streaming services, mobile apps) that transitioned the entire economy.

Incumbents who fail to recognize that their highly profitable “Custom Built” component is being commoditized will fight against the transition, only to be crushed by a disruptor leveraging the new utility to provide greater value elsewhere. A successful transition manager anticipates this drift, maps the terrain, and cannibalizes their own legacy products before someone else does.
