108: Crossing the Chasm

June 16, 2026

While Rogers’ Diffusion of Innovations model presents a smooth bell curve of adoption, Geoffrey Moore identified a fatal flaw in how companies interpret it. The transition from one adopter category to the next is not seamless. There are gaps between the psychologies of each group.

The most lethal of these gaps lies between the Early Adopters (Visionaries) and the Early Majority (Pragmatists). Moore coined this terrifying void “The Chasm.”

Why the Chasm Exists

Visionaries buy technology to gain a massive competitive leap. They expect bugs, they want custom integrations, and they don’t care what other people are doing.

Pragmatists, however, buy technology to solve a known problem reliably. They demand rock-solid references from other pragmatists, they want a complete “whole product” solution (with support, third-party add-ons, and guarantees), and they absolutely refuse to buy from a company that might go out of business next year.

Because pragmatists will not buy without references from other pragmatists, a paradox emerges: how do you sell to the first pragmatist? This is where countless disruptive technologies die.

Suggested Reading:

  • Crossing the Chasm by Geoffrey A. Moore (1991). The definitive guide to marketing and selling high-tech products to mainstream customers.

The Chasm Bridge Builder

To cross the chasm, you cannot attack the whole market at once. You must build a bridge using the "Bowling Pin" strategy.

The Bowling Alley Strategy

To cross the chasm, Moore argues you must abandon the desire to sell to everyone. Instead, you must target a single, highly specific niche market (the head bowling pin) that has a desperate, unfulfilled need. You must dominate this niche completely, creating a 100% complete “whole product” for them.

Once you dominate that specific niche, those pragmatists will serve as rock-solid references for the adjacent niche market (the next bowling pin). By knocking over one adjacent niche at a time, you build a bridge of references across the chasm. Once enough niches are conquered, the broader pragmatist market views you as the safe, de facto standard, and mass adoption (the “Tornado”) begins.
