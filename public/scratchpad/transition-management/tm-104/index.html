104: Blue Ocean Strategy

June 16, 2026

Disruptive Innovation explains why incumbents fail, but it doesn’t necessarily provide a proactive framework for designing a market-beating product from scratch. For this, transition management turns to Blue Ocean Strategy, developed by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne.

Their research, based on a study of 150 strategic moves spanning more than 100 years and 30 industries, challenges the fundamental tenets of classical competitive strategy. Traditional strategy dictates that a company must either compete on lower costs or compete on superior differentiation—but it cannot do both. Blue Ocean Strategy dismantles this structuralist view, arguing that market boundaries and industry structures are not given; they can be systematically reconstructed by the actions and beliefs of industry players.

Red Oceans vs. Blue Oceans

Red Oceans represent all the industries in existence today—the known market space. In red oceans, industry boundaries are defined and accepted, and the competitive rules of the game are known. Here, companies try to outperform their rivals to grab a greater share of existing demand. As the market space gets crowded, prospects for profits and growth are reduced. Products become commodities, and cutthroat competition turns the ocean bloody (hence, “red”).

Blue Oceans, in contrast, denote all the industries not in existence today—the unknown market space, untainted by competition. In blue oceans, demand is created rather than fought over. There is ample opportunity for growth that is both profitable and rapid. In blue oceans, competition is irrelevant because the rules of the game are waiting to be set.

Suggested Reading:

  • Blue Ocean Strategy by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne (2004). The foundational text explaining how to create uncontested market space and make the competition irrelevant.

Value Innovation

The cornerstone of Blue Ocean Strategy is Value Innovation. Instead of focusing on beating the competition (which usually involves a trade-off between value and cost), value innovation focuses on making the competition irrelevant by creating a leap in value for buyers, thereby opening up new and uncontested market space.

Value innovation is achieved through the simultaneous pursuit of differentiation and low cost. It asks companies to critically evaluate the factors an industry takes for granted and eliminate them, while creating or raising factors the industry has never offered.

Reconstructing Market Boundaries

Transitions often happen when a company successfully reconstructs market boundaries to create a blue ocean. Cirque du Soleil did not attempt to compete with Ringling Bros. by securing better animal acts. Instead, they eliminated animal acts entirely, introduced elements of theater and artistic music, and created a new blue ocean that appealed to adults willing to pay premium ticket prices, completely transitioning the concept of the circus. By doing so, they rendered the traditional competitive factors of the circus industry obsolete.
