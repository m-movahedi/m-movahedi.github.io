112: Transition Governance

June 16, 2026

    We have reached the culmination of our series. We have mapped the landscape (MLP, Wardley), engineered the disruption (S-Curves, Blue Ocean), manipulated the psychology of adoption (Diffusion, JTBD), and mitigated institutional risks (SNM, Ecosystems, Inertia).

    But how does a society or a massive organization actively steer a long-term transition? This requires Transition Governance.

    Steer, Don’t Control

    Transition Governance acknowledges that massive socio-technical shifts (like the transition to renewable energy or the adoption of AI) are too complex, too wicked, and too non-linear to be managed through traditional top-down planning. You cannot use a rigid Gantt chart to manage a 30-year societal shift.

    Instead, governance relies on Adaptive Management and continuous feedback loops. The role of the governor (whether a CEO or a policymaker) is not to pick the single winning technology and enforce it, but to define the ultimate goal (e.g., “Zero Emissions”) and stimulate a diverse portfolio of competing niches. As the transition unfolds, the governor monitors the landscape, amplifies the niches that gain traction, phases out the failing ones, and applies pressure to the incumbent regime to accelerate the shift.

    Suggested Reading:

    • Transition management: a reflexive governance approach by Derk Loorbach (2010). The foundational text defining how reflexive governance is applied to societal transitions.

    Conclusion

    Transition Management is not a prescriptive formula; it is a lens. By understanding the macro pressures (MLP), mapping the terrain (Wardley), targeting the right psychology (JTBD, Diffusion), and applying reflexive governance, leaders can stop reacting to disruptive transitions and start orchestrating them.
