110: Ecosystem Building

June 16, 2026

The history of technology is littered with brilliant, objectively superior innovations that utterly failed to transition the market. Often, this happens not because the core technology was flawed, but because the innovation ecosystem surrounding it was unready.

Ron Adner’s work on Innovation Ecosystems emphasizes that your success no longer depends solely on your own efforts. It depends on the success of other partners who must innovate alongside you. If you build a revolutionary electric vehicle (the core innovation), but no one builds the high-speed charging network (the co-innovation), your product fails.

Co-Innovation Risk and Adoption Chain Risk

Transition managers must calculate two hidden risks that doom isolated technologies:

  1. Co-Innovation Risk: The extent to which the success of your innovation depends on the successful commercialization of other innovations. (e.g., HD Televisions were useless until broadcasters adopted HD cameras and transmission standards).
  2. Adoption Chain Risk: The extent to which partners will need to adopt your innovation before end-consumers can even evaluate it. (e.g., For a new medical device to reach a patient, it must be adopted by the FDA, the hospital purchasing board, the insurance company, and the doctor).

Suggested Reading:

  • The Wide Lens: What Successful Innovators See That Others Miss by Ron Adner (2012). A masterclass on how to map your ecosystem and mitigate the hidden risks of collaborative innovation.

Ecosystem Bottlenecks

The core innovation (Electric Vehicle) cannot succeed until the co-innovation bottlenecks are resolved.

🔋 Battery Range
🔌 Charging Grid
🚗 EV Market

Minimum Viable Ecosystem (MVE)

Because of these dependencies, transition management requires building a Minimum Viable Ecosystem (MVE) before launching a Minimum Viable Product (MVP).

An MVE represents the smallest configuration of partners required to create a commercially viable offering. Apple’s iPod succeeded where early MP3 players failed because Apple didn’t just build the hardware (the MVP); they built iTunes and secured the legal rights to the music catalogs, resolving the critical co-innovation bottleneck. They built the ecosystem, and by doing so, transitioned the entire music industry.
