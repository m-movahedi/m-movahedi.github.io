107: Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)

June 16, 2026

While the Diffusion of Innovations model explains who adopts a technology and when, it does not explain the underlying psychological causality of why they adopt it. Why do people abandon a product they have used for years to switch to something completely new?

To answer this, transition management utilizes the Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) theory, popularized by Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta. The core premise is that customers do not buy products; they “hire” products to do a “job” they need to get done in their lives. If a new technology does the job significantly better, they will “fire” the old product and hire the new one.

The Forces of Progress

Transitioning a customer from an incumbent product to a disruptive product is an uphill psychological battle. Moesta identifies four distinct psychological forces that dictate whether a customer will switch (make progress) or stay put.

Two forces promote the transition:

  1. Push of the Situation: The frustration or pain with the current way of doing things.
  2. Pull of the New Idea: The magnetic appeal and promise of the new solution.

Two forces block the transition: 3. Habit of the Present: The comfort of familiarity (“I know how this works”). 4. Anxiety of the New: The fear of the unknown (“What if it breaks? What if I look stupid?”).

Suggested Reading:

  • Competing Against Luck: The Story of Innovation and Customer Choice by Clayton Christensen et al. (2016). Explains the JTBD framework and how to uncover the hidden reasons customers make choices.

Applying JTBD to Transition Management

Incumbents often fail because they obsess over improving their product’s demographic features or statistical performance without realizing the fundamental “Job” has changed.

Conversely, attackers succeed by targeting non-consumption. They find people who have a powerful “Push” (unmet need) but face massive “Anxiety” or “Habit” barriers with the incumbent solutions. By designing a product that perfectly aligns with the required Job—and explicitly engineering the marketing to reduce anxiety—the attacker rapidly captures the market. Understanding the JTBD is the key to accelerating the Diffusion of Innovations.
